Electromechanical coupling in polaronic ceria

Ceria should be a poor electromechanical material: low permittivity, high stiffness, centrosymmetric. Yet doped ceria shows electrostriction more than a hundred times above the empirical rule, usually credited to oxygen vacancies. Here, nanocrystalline Gd- and Ca-doped ceria films, with vacancies frozen at grain boundaries, still reach M33 above 10−16 m2 V−2, with the same response for both dopants. Conduction is electronic, by Ce3+ small polarons, and each hop swells a cerium ion from 0.97 to 1.14 Å. Cation-site defects, not vacancies alone, carry the coupling.

Giant electrostriction in disordered films

Cantilevers driven at about 100 kV cm−1 with less than 10 V, under dc bias, were fitted with a finite-element model that includes the Maxwell stress. The Gd film gives |M33| = 5.3 × 10−16 and a much smaller M31. In the Ca film M31 (2.3 × 10−19) is large enough to dominate the bending. The response weakens above 700 Hz and vanishes above 5 kHz.

The dopant no longer matters

In bulk ceramics, Gd-doped ceria relaxes at low frequency while Ca-doped ceria is frequency independent, a difference linked to dopant–vacancy association. In these films the two behave alike, so another mechanism dominates.

Expansion along the field, contraction across it

Synchrotron diffraction during a 0–15 V ramp shows no phase change, but anisotropic strain: the lattice expands parallel to the field and contracts in plane, in both compositions. This rules out isotropic causes such as Joule heating or global reduction and links the strain to M33 and M31.

Electrons hop, ions stay put

Impedance shows n-type electronic conduction by small-polaron hopping, with no significant oxygen-ion contribution. Bias raises carrier mobility, reversibly in the Gd film and with non-volatile switching in the Ca film. XRD and Raman show expanded lattices and strong defect bands, pointing to a high Ce3+ population stabilized by the nanograins.

A polaronic route to giant electrostriction

Each hop changes the size of a cation, so charge transport carries a local strain. The work proposes that dynamic electro-elastic sites on the cation lattice, Ce3+ polarons and dopants, drive the coupling together with oxygen vacancies, a design route for lead-free electromechanical oxides.
