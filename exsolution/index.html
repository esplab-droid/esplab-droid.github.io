Hierarchical exsolution in vertically aligned heterostructures

Exsolution grows metal nanoparticles out of a host oxide under reducing conditions, anchored to the lattice they come from. In single-phase films, much of the metal stays trapped inside. We grew a self-assembled film of two oxides, a Ni-doped perovskite and a fluorite, arranged as vertical nanocolumns. The vertical interfaces between the columns carry Ni to the surface faster and more selectively than the bulk.

A self-assembled nano-checkerboard

The perovskite (SPTNO) and fluorite (CGO) phases separate into uniform columns through the whole film thickness, with coherent vertical interfaces. The SPTNO columns carry a nominal +1.7% out-of-plane tensile strain.

1.40 vs 3.68 eVDFT Ni migration barrier,
interface vs perovskite bulk

Interfaces are the fast pathway

Calculations show Ni moves far more easily along the perovskite–fluorite interface than through the perovskite lattice. Nucleation happens at the surface, at the column boundaries.

≈ 80 vs ≈ 33 µm−2particle density after 1 min
at 650 °C, VAN vs single phase

More particles, faster

One minute of reduction gives about 2.5 times the particle density of a conventional epitaxial film, with particles around 17 nm.

≈ 67 vs ≈ 120 nmparticle size after 12 h
at 650 °C

Smaller particles, none trapped, structure intact

No Ni particles remain trapped inside the VAN film, while the single-phase film retains them in the bulk. The columnar order and the film density survive the reduction.
