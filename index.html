Functional metal oxides for energy conversion, electromechanics and ionic devices

Our group studies how point defects, interfaces and three-dimensional architecture set the properties of functional metal oxides. We work from defective bulk oxides to epitaxial thin films and engineered heterostructures, and on to solid oxide and protonic ceramic cells. This page collects recent results and the questions currently open in the lab.

Research

Defects, thin films and heterostructures: the common thread.

Many of the properties we study do not come from the ideal lattice. They come from oxygen vacancies, polarons and dopant–vacancy pairs, and from what happens to them under strain or at an interface. We grow epitaxial films and multilayers to control these defects at the nanometre scale, and we compare them with bulk ceramics of the same composition.

Defective metal oxides

Heterostructures

Multilayers and bilayers where lattice discontinuity or a buried polarization reorganizes the defects of the adjacent layer, giving properties neither material has alone.

Electrostriction-driven phase instability enables giant pseudo-piezoelectricity in Hf0.5Zr0.5O2

Science Advances, 2026. First author Achilles Bergne, with partners in Aveiro, Lund, Stuttgart, Jülich and Riga.

Epitaxial hafnium zirconium oxide films are giant electrostrictors (M ≈ 10−14 m2 V−2). Above 24 kV cm−1 the electrostrictive strain drives the lattice into a polar phase, which switches on a large, reversible piezoelectric-like response in a CMOS-compatible oxide.

Electro-chemo-mechanical coupling in Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 ferroionic heterostructures

Advanced Functional Materials, 2026. First author Achilles Bergne, with partners in Aveiro, Málaga, Lund and Riga.

Epitaxial HZO grown on Gd-doped ceria or yttria-stabilized zirconia, instead of LSMO, exchanges oxygen vacancies with the buffer across a coherent fluorite–fluorite interface. The exchange biases the polymorph balance, strengthens polarization and piezoresponse, and lowers leakage, while in situ nano-XRD shows the polar phase holding up to ±200 kV mm−1.

Hole polaronic confinement in (111) yttria-stabilised zirconia

Small, 2026. First author Milica Vasiljevic, with partners in Rome, Trieste, Málaga, Tampere and Lund.

In epitaxial YSZ films below about 20 nm, Y3+ segregation orders the oxygen vacancies at the (111) surface. Filling them leaves holes self-trapped on surface oxygen as O− polarons, giving p-type mixed conduction and ten times stronger electrostriction in the benchmark ionic conductor.

Monolithic gyroidal solid oxide cells by additive manufacturing

Nature Energy, 2025. With DTU Construct.

An 8YSZ solid oxide cell printed as a single gyroid lattice, without metallic interconnects or stack seals. The architecture gives uniform gas and heat distribution. Cells withstood 100 °C thermal swings and repeated switching between SOFC and SOEC operation. Scale-up is in progress under the name GYROX.

Enhanced non-classical electrostriction in strained tetragonal ceria

Nature Communications, 2025.

Positive tetragonality in compressively strained ceria films orients the defect-related elastic dipoles and Ce3+ centres, raising the electrostriction coefficient well above values for unstrained material.

Electromechanical coupling in polaronic ceria

Journal of Physics: Energy, 2025. First author Victor Buratto Tinti, with DTU Physics and Lund University / MAX IV. JPhys Energy EL Award 2025.

Room-temperature sputtered ceria films with ~10 nm grains keep giant electrostriction although their oxygen vacancies are frozen, with the same response for Gd and Ca doping. Conduction is by Ce3+ small polarons, and the size change at each hop couples charge and strain, pointing to cation-site defects as a driver of the effect.

Tunable ferroionic properties in CeO2/BaTiO3 heterostructures

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 2024. Equal first authors Milica Vasiljevic and Francesco Chiabrera, with partners in Aveiro, Trieste, Barcelona and Málaga.

A 5 nm CeO2−δ layer on a 10 nm BaTiO3 film replicates the buried polarization through reconfiguration of oxygen vacancies and small polarons. The polarity sets the surface redox character and the water adsorption and splitting behaviour.

Hierarchical exsolution in vertically aligned heterostructures

Nature Communications, 2024. First author Javier Zamudio-García, with Universidad de Málaga.

A self-assembled film of Ni-doped SrTiO3-based perovskite and Gd-doped CeO2 nanocolumns. The vertical interfaces carry Ni to the surface, giving about 2.5 times more nanoparticles than a single-phase film after one minute at 650 °C, with none trapped inside.

Journal of Materials Chemistry A, 2024.

Unlike trivalent-doped ceria, whose response decays above 1 to 10 Hz, divalent Ca doping gives a frequency-stable electrostrictive strain, attributed to strongly bound Ca–vacancy pairs.

Atomically engineered interfaces yield extraordinary electrostriction

Nature, 2022. With DTU Energy colleagues and partners in Belgium, Israel and Switzerland.

Alternating epitaxial layers of gadolinia-doped CeO2 and erbia-stabilized δ-Bi2O3 on NdGaO3. Coherent strain from the lattice discontinuity at the interfaces raises electrostriction far above values for simple oxides, which are normally below 10−19 m2 V−2.
