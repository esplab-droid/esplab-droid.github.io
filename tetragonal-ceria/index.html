Enhanced non-classical electrostriction in strained tetragonal ceria

Gd-doped ceria is a strong electrostrictor because its oxygen vacancies and Ce3+ ions form defect complexes that distort the lattice locally. Growing thin films on different substrates sets the in-plane lattice and lets the film answer out of plane. Under in-plane compression the films become tetragonal (c/a > 1), the defect dipoles line up out of plane, and an in-plane field swings them over together. The in-plane coefficient M11 reaches about 3.6 × 10−15 m2 V−2, more than ten times that of tensile or unstrained films.

Compression gives the largest response

Thin films with positive tetragonality reach the highest in-plane coefficient, more than an order of magnitude above films under tension or near-zero strain. The films are grown on 0.1 mm substrates used as cantilevers, with planar gold electrodes. A 1 Hz field along the CGO [110] direction drives the M11 mode, and the displacement is read by laser interferometry at the second harmonic. Stress scales linearly with E2.

The substrate sets the strain

The nominal mismatch with CGO is +1.6 % on SrTiO3 (tensile), about 0.7 % on NdGaO3 (quasi-null) and −1.1 % on LaAlO3 (compressive). The measured in-plane strain is smaller because the films partly relax out of plane: for CGO20 it is +0.166 %, about zero and −0.240 %. Compression produces positive tetragonality, tension negative.

Compression orients the defect dipoles

In compressed films the ceria F2g Raman mode splits strongly, and XANES shows more Ce3+. Strain reconfigures the vacancy complexes into more polarizable Ce3+–vacancy pairs whose elastic dipoles align out of plane. An in-plane field rotates this aligned population, giving a large in-plane strain. Ce3+ polarons may add to the response beyond the vacancies.

dipole moment (DFT)

Calculations agree

DFT on strained CeO2 supercells with an oxygen vacancy finds that, under compression, the dipole moment is largest out of plane, about twice its in-plane components. The enhancement under compression was predicted before, but this is the first experimental evidence for the M11 mode.

Tetragonality as a descriptor

Substrate-induced strain matters far more than dopant or vacancy content, although thin CGO10 gives about twice the response of CGO20. Thickness sets the limit, because thicker films relax and lose the gain. The response decreases between 1 and 100 Hz but stays within the same order of magnitude.
