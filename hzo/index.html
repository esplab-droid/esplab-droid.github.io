Electrostriction-driven phase instability enables giant pseudo-piezoelectricity in Hf0.5Zr0.5O2

Hafnium zirconium oxide is already used in silicon chips, but in its stable monoclinic form it is not piezoelectric. Epitaxial HZO films turn out to be exceptionally strong electrostrictors. Above 24 kV cm−1 the electrostrictive strain itself pushes the lattice into a polar orthorhombic phase, and the resulting polarization acts as an internal bias that turns the quadratic response into a large linear one. The mechanism is extrinsic: it comes from strain-driven phase instability, not from intrinsic ferroelectric switching.

Findings

Key quantity on the left.

M ≈ 1 × 10−14m2 V−2, 10 nm films
on YSZ and SrTiO3

The strongest electrostriction in a single-layer oxide

The second-harmonic strain grows quadratically with field and is larger than the first-harmonic response. Pure HfO2 films on the same substrate show no response, so Zr is required. Even quasi-amorphous HZO films are strong enough for a 30 nm layer to crack its 0.5 mm substrate.

24 kV cm−1activation field;
≈ 6% strain along [010]

Strain flips the lattice to a polar phase

DFT shows the polar orthorhombic Pca21 phase becomes the most stable above 6% strain along [010], with a change from sevenfold to sixfold cation coordination. In situ XRD shows a step in lattice strain at the same field where interferometry sees the first-order response switch on. The structural change is elastic and reversible.

2,900 to 4,050pm V−1 effective dxx after
activation, on YSZ and SrTiO3

A piezoelectric-like response switched on by the field

Below the threshold the first-order response is close to zero; above it the stress grows linearly with field. Switching-spectroscopy PFM confirms a nonpolar-to-polar transformation at the nanoscale.

> 10,000pm V−1 pseudo-piezoelectric
coefficient with dc bias

Linearized electrostriction

Superimposing a dc bias on the ac field linearizes the electrostrictive response. The resulting coefficients exceed the previous record for Gd-doped CeO2 and the best bulk piezoceramics and relaxors. The response is strongest at low frequency, relevant for actuators, adaptive metasurfaces, acoustofluidics and reconfigurable NEMS.
