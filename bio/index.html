About

Vincenzo Esposito is Professor of Ceramic Science and Engineering at DTU Energy, the Department of Energy Conversion and Storage of the Technical University of Denmark, in Kongens Lyngby. He leads ESPLab, a group working on functional metal oxides for energy conversion, electromechanics and ionic devices.

His research sits between nanoionics, solid-state chemistry and advanced materials processing. A recurring theme is that the useful properties of an oxide often come from its defects rather than from the ideal lattice. Confining highly defective oxides at the nanoscale opens new stability domains and lets their electrical and electromechanical properties be tuned; the arrangement of oxygen defects in nano-domains can produce very large electro-chemo-mechanical responses in materials such as cerium oxide.

The group combines thin films and heterostructures grown by pulsed laser deposition with ceramic additive manufacturing, including gyroid and other triply periodic minimal surface architectures printed as complete solid oxide cells and piezoelectric devices.
