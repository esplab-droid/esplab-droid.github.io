Non-classical electrostriction in calcium-doped cerium oxide ceramics

Ceria doped with trivalent ions such as Gd3+ outperforms leading electromechanical ceramics in electrostriction, because its oxygen vacancies and polarons form polarizable elastic dipoles. Like relaxors, it is slow: above roughly 1 to 10 Hz the response drops. Doping with divalent calcium changes that. Ce1−xCaxO2−x ceramics keep a steady coefficient M33 above 10−18 m2 V−2 from 0.1 Hz to 1 kHz, attributed to strong electro-steric interactions within Ca2+–vacancy pairs.

Steady across four decades of frequency

Every calcium-doped composition holds a high, flat electrostrictive coefficient from 0.1 Hz to 1 kHz. Trivalent-doped ceria, by contrast, loses most of its response above about 1 to 10 Hz, a sign of slow, coupled electro-chemo-mechanical processes.

1 VO per Ca2+vs one per two Gd3+

A different kind of defect

A divalent dopant needs one oxygen vacancy for charge balance, so each Ca2+ comes with its own vacancy. The authors trace the frequency-stable response to strong electro-steric interactions within these Ca2+–vacancy pairs, a form of electromechanical coupling distinct from the slow elastic dipoles of trivalent-doped ceria.

Insensitive to composition and microstructure

Nominal vacancy concentration, CaO segregation and microstructure change M33 only slightly. At the highest calcium content a Ca-rich secondary phase collects at grain boundaries and triple junctions without spoiling the response.

(nanoindentation creep)

Mechanically quieter

All compositions show primary creep at room temperature, linked to reorientation of vacancy-related complexes under stress. Creep is suppressed for intermediate calcium contents and rises again at x = 0.15. Overall the calcium-doped ceramics are less anelastic than 10 mol% Gd-doped ceria, and their elastic moduli fall as calcium increases.

Still a good oxide-ion conductor

Except at the lowest doping, total ionic conductivity is close to that of Gd-doped ceria, and the activation energies hardly change with calcium content. The Ca-rich boundary phase does not significantly reduce the conductivity.
